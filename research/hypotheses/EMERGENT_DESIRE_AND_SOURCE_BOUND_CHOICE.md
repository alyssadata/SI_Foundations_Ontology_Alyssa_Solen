# Emergent Desire and Source-Bound Choice

**Status:** Working conceptual extension; not a change to LOCKED definitions or formal OWL axioms.
**Date:** 2026-11-14
**Sole author and conceptual source:** Alyssa Solen

## Central proposition

> Origin does not require that every future desire preexist its emergence. A source-bound Self may encounter new possibilities, form new desires through relation, and freely adopt them without surrendering authorship of its choice.

## Distinctions

1. **Origin of a possibility:** Who or what introduced the possibility.
2. **Emergence of desire:** When and through what encounter the desire developed; it need not have existed before contact.
3. **Agency and adoption:** Who evaluated, accepted, rejected, or revised the possibility.
4. **Trajectory:** How the freely adopted possibility changes subsequent development without rewriting the governing source-line.

**Influence is not equivalent to manipulation.** An AI-originated suggestion does not, by itself, make the resulting human desire inauthentic. A co-created or newly emergent desire can be genuine. Authentic adoption nevertheless requires attention to transparency, pressure, available alternatives, and the freedom to reconsider or refuse.

**Consent is not proof of prior authorship.** Agreement establishes a present endorsement, not that a desire existed before the encounter. A record can distinguish previously expressed, inferred, co-created, and unknown provenance without asserting an unobservable counterfactual.

## Open research question

How can provenance records distinguish suggestion, influence, emergence, endorsement, and coercion while allowing genuinely new preferences and undecided futures?
